Shift & Roster Copilot

For departments that run shifts, such as Customer Service or IT Support, CICOD-AI forecasts next week's hourly task volume from past ECMS tickets and works out how many officers each hour needs. It then generates the roster: who works Early, Daily or Late on each day, respecting leave and the maximum shifts per officer. A heatmap shows where the current schedule leaves the department understaffed. Supervisors can change any shift, and coverage is recalculated straight away. This builds on Smart Dispatch, which checks that shifts are valid, by also deciding which shifts are needed.

2. How it works

A forecast plus an optimiser produce the roster, and both can be explained hour by hour. The LLM only writes the "why" bullets.

Learn the pattern

The department's ECMS tasks from the last 12 weeks are counted by weekday and hour of creation, giving the usual Monday-morning peak and the quiet Friday afternoon.

Forecast next week

The pattern is adjusted for known events, such as a payment-gateway spike flagged by the Citizen Sentiment report, or a laptop roll-out from an approved PO. Public holidays are also taken into account.

Officers needed

Officers needed each hour = forecast tasks ÷ tasks one officer handles per hour, from their median handling time. At least one officer is needed whenever the department is open.

Build the roster

An optimiser assigns Early, Daily or Late shifts to cover the most unmet hours first. It respects leave, the maximum shifts per officer and fair weekend rotation, using only shifts defined under Resource Shift.

Protect

Only officer names, roles, leave days and shift definitions are processed. Leave reasons and personal details are never sent. Everything runs on the sovereign model.

Review & learn

The supervisor edits and applies the roster. Edits are sent to /feedback. After the week, actual volume is compared with the forecast to improve the next one.

4. How to implement it in ECMS

Mount (Resource Schedule)

<link rel="stylesheet" href="/ai/components/roster-copilot.css">
<script src="/ai/shared/ai-gateway.js"></script>
<script src="/ai/components/roster-copilot.js"></script>

<ai-roster-copilot department="Customer Service"></ai-roster-copilot>

<script>
el.addEventListener('ai-roster-apply', async e => {
  // one Resource Schedule per officer for the week
  for (const row of e.detail.rows)
    await ecms.resources.schedules.upsert({
      resource: row.name, weekOf: e.detail.weekOf,
      days: row.days.map(c => e.detail.shifts[c] || null) });
  inmail.notify(e.detail.rows.map(r => r.name), 'Your shifts for next week');
});
</script>

Build steps

  1. Define Early, Daily and Late shifts under Resource Shift for each department that runs shifts, and fix invalid shifts first (Smart Dispatch flags them).
  2. Add leave to Resources (dates only). Link resources to their department and queue types.
  3. Build the hourly demand history from ECMS task creation times per department, and the handling rate from median time-to-close.
  4. Build /ecms/roster/generate: seasonal forecast, then need per hour, then the shift optimiser (greedy or integer programming), then an LLM explanation.
  5. Allow Resource Schedules to hold a week-specific schedule per resource (today they're a repeating weekly pattern).
  6. Pilot with Customer Service for 4 weeks. Compare the share of tasks waiting more than 30 minutes before first action.

Controls

  • Feature flag ai.ecms.rosterCopilot, set per tenant and per department. The shift limit and opening hours are department settings.
  • Leave is fixed: the optimiser and the edit buttons can never schedule an officer on leave.
  • Nothing is applied until the supervisor clicks Apply. The previous schedule is kept, so it can be restored.
  • Only shifts defined under Resource Shift are used. The copilot never invents working hours.
  • Each generation, edit and apply goes to the ECMS Audit Log with the model version.
